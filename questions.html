Questions & Answers

Short, plain answers to common questions. For anything official, check your county elections office or the Texas Secretary of State.

Can I vote by phone or online?

No. In Texas you vote on paper — in person or by mail.

Where do I actually vote? Can I vote from home?

You vote in person at a polling place — a building near you — or by mail only if you qualify. You cannot vote from home by phone or internet.

Why can’t I use my phone in the polling place?

Texas law bans phones and any device with a camera within 100 feet of the voting machines. It keeps your vote private and stops anyone from being paid or pressured to vote a certain way. Look things up before you go, and bring paper notes.

Do I have to pick a party?

Only in the primary. The November general election is open to all registered voters.

Do I have to vote on everything on the ballot?

No. Skip anything you do not know. The rest of your ballot still counts.

I have a green card, work permit, or visa — can I vote?

No. Only United States citizens can register or vote. Doing so as a non-citizen is a serious crime and can hurt your immigration case. When in doubt, ask before registering.

Is my vote secret?

Yes. No one is told how you voted.

Do I need to bring my voter registration card?

No — bring an accepted photo ID. The card is not required to vote.

What if my name isn’t on the list when I arrive?

Do not leave — ask for a provisional ballot and let the workers help sort it out.

I don’t have a car, or I work all day — can I still vote?

Yes. Early voting runs for many days, and there are many locations. In Travis County you can vote at any vote center.

I moved. Am I still registered?

Update your registration, especially if you moved to a new county. Check the “Am I Registered?” tool before Election Day.

What if I’m in line when the polls close?

If you are in line by closing time, you can still vote. Stay in line.

Someone told me the wrong day or place — who do I trust?

Only your county elections office and the Texas Secretary of State. See Get More Help.
