Know the Election Days

In the United States, there is more than one Election Day. Different votes happen on different days. Here are the main ones.

The primary — the “tryouts”

Each team (we call teams parties) picks its player first. You usually vote in only one party’s primary.

The general election — the “championship game”

The winners from each party face off. This is the big one, in November.

A runoff — “overtime”

Sometimes nobody wins clearly. Then the top two go to overtime — a runoff — on a later day.

How often?

  • The President is chosen only every 4 years.
  • Many other offices — like City Council and the State Legislature — are voted on more often.
  • Austin City Council races often go to a runoff.

⚠ Watch out — common mistakes

  • Show up for the right election. There is more than one election day — check which one is next for you.
  • City Council has no party primary. You vote for City Council in November (and a December runoff if no one wins over half). You do not need to vote in a spring party primary to vote for City Council. Other local races have no party either — city offices, school board, and community college board — and ballot questions (yes-or-no propositions and bonds). (Judges and county offices in Texas are partisan, so those do have primaries.)
  • A runoff means voting again. Your November vote does not carry over — you must come back and vote in the runoff.
  • Dates change every cycle. Trust the dates above and the official calendar, not your memory. Add them to your calendar if you have one.
